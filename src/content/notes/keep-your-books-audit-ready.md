Many businesses treat their accounts as a once-a-year clean-up. That approach turns tax filing and audit season into a scramble. A handful of monthly habits makes the difference.

## A simple monthly routine

1. **Record everything promptly.** Post sales, purchases and expenses as they happen, with the supporting bill or voucher attached.
2. **Reconcile bank accounts every month.** Differences are easy to trace within weeks and very hard to trace after a year.
3. **Review receivables and payables.** Follow up on overdue customers and confirm what you owe suppliers.
4. **Check your tax positions.** Match GST returns and TDS deposits with your books before the due dates, not after.
5. **Review a short management report.** A one-page view of sales, expenses, cash and dues helps you spot problems early.

## Keep personal and business money apart

Mixing personal and business transactions makes accounts unreliable and raises questions in an audit or tax assessment. Use separate bank accounts and cards, and treat any owner withdrawals or contributions as proper entries.

## Organise your documents

Keep invoices, contracts, bank statements and tax filings in one organised place — digital copies are fine if they are complete and easy to find. Know how long each type of record must be retained.

## Why it pays off

Regular books give you reliable numbers for decisions, faster loan or funding approvals, fewer notices and a shorter, cheaper audit. If your team does not have time for this, **outsourced accounting** or a part-time finance function can take it off your plate.
