Every year, salaried taxpayers are asked the same question: should I stay with the old tax regime or move to the new one? The honest answer is that it depends on your numbers, not on what a colleague chose.

## Start with the deductions you really use

The old regime allows a long list of deductions and exemptions — investments under section 80C, health insurance premiums, interest on a home loan, house rent allowance and more. The new regime offers lower slab rates but allows far fewer deductions.

So the first step is to add up what you **actually** claim, not what is possible in theory. If you rarely invest or pay rent, the new regime is often simpler and cheaper. If you have a home loan, pay significant rent in a high-rent city or already invest heavily, the old regime may still win.

## Compare, don't guess

The quickest way to decide is to run your own figures through both regimes side by side. Our [income tax calculator](/tools/income-tax-calculator) does this in a few seconds and shows which regime gives the lower tax for your income and deductions.

## Things people forget

- **Your employer asks early.** Employers usually ask for your regime choice at the start of the year to compute TDS. You can still change at the time of filing your return, but choosing well early avoids over-deduction.
- **It can change every year.** For salaried individuals the choice is generally not permanent, so review it when your income, loans or investments change.
- **Business income is different.** People with business or professional income have more limited ability to switch between regimes, so the decision needs more care.
- **Future plans matter.** If you plan to buy a home, take a loan or start investing, the answer may change next year.
