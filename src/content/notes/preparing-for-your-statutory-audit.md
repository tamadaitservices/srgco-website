A statutory audit goes faster, costs less effort and raises fewer queries when the business is prepared. Most delays come from missing paperwork, not from difficult accounting. Here is a practical way to prepare.

## Close your books properly

- Make sure every month is posted and reconciled up to the year-end.
- Reconcile **all bank accounts**, including loan and overdraft accounts, to the statements.
- Clear or explain old, unadjusted entries in suspense and advance accounts.

## Get your supporting records together

- Bank statements, loan statements and **confirmations of balances** from lenders.
- Ledger confirmations or statements from major customers and suppliers.
- Purchase and sales documents, expense vouchers and payroll records for the period.
- A **fixed asset register** with additions, disposals and depreciation workings.
- Stock records and the method used to value closing stock.

## Reconcile your tax and statutory positions

- GST returns reconciled with the books (sales, purchases and input credit).
- TDS deducted, deposited and filed — with certificates issued.
- Provident fund, ESI and other statutory dues paid and reconciled.
- Advance tax and income tax payments recorded.

## Prepare the company-law documents

For companies and LLPs, keep ready the minutes of board and general meetings, statutory registers, and details of related-party transactions, directors' interests and borrowings.

## Agree a timeline early

Share a simple schedule with your auditor: when records will be ready, who is the contact for each area, and when queries will be answered. A short kick-off conversation saves days later.
